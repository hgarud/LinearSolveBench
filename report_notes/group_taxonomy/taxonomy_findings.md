# How to interpret SuiteSparse Matrix Collection groups

## Executive conclusion

A SuiteSparse **group is primarily a provenance namespace**, not a mathematical
matrix class.  The older official `UFgui` help is unusually explicit: `Group`
indicates the source of a problem—“a person, organization, or other
collection”—and `Group/Name` is its full name.  The current statistics page
likewise says that `Group/Name`, not `Name` alone, is the unique identifier.

That explains the naming.  `Averous`, `Bai`, `Goodwin`, and many others are
contributors; `Boeing`, `Sandia`, `IBM_EDA`, and `TAMU_SmartGridCenter` are
organizations or labs; `HB`, `LPnetlib`, `SNAP`, `LAW`, `Pajek`, and `DIMACS10`
are imported collections/benchmarks; `DRIVCAV`, `TOKAMAK`, `VLSI`, and
`FlowIPM22` identify an application or construction.  A group can be a clean
parameter sequence, a domain-specific submission, or a historical bundle.
Consequently, membership is useful context, but it never guarantees equal
shape, symmetry, scalar type, definiteness, or even application kind.

The current public CSV index has 2,904 matrices in 203 groups and reports a
`LastRevisionDate` of 31-Oct-2023 18:12:37.  Its only observed curator-label
difference from the current official website source is `Chevron1`–`Chevron4`:
the CSV says `other problem`, while the current site says `2D/3D problem`; the
generated outputs use the current label.  The accompanying exhaustive table is
in `group_catalogue.md`; machine-readable values and the full official notes
are in `group_metrics.csv` and `group_metrics.json`.

## What the main kinds of group names mean

These categories describe the naming/provenance convention, not disjoint
matrix taxonomic classes.

| Naming pattern | What the group represents | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Contributor or editor | Matrices submitted or assembled by a person/team; the applications can be narrow or broad | `Averous`, `Bai`, `Goodwin`, `Grund`, `Rothberg`, `Simon`, `Williams` |
| Organization, company, or lab | Problems generated by one institution/toolchain | `Boeing`, `Sandia`, `IBM_EDA`, `Freescale`, `NASA`, `TAMU_SmartGridCenter`, `VDOL` |
| Imported archive/benchmark | A pre-existing test collection; often intentionally broad | `HB` (Harwell–Boeing), `GHS_indef`, `GHS_psdef`, `Oberwolfach`, `LPnetlib`, `Qaplib` |
| Graph/network corpus | Adjacency or weighted-adjacency matrices from a data repository or challenge | `Pajek`, `SNAP`, `LAW`, `DIMACS10`, `Gset`, `GAP`, `AG-Monien`, `ML_Graph` |
| Application/code/construction | Matrices sharing a simulation, code, equation, or generative construction | `FIDAP`, `DRIVCAV`, `TOKAMAK`, `VLSI`, `FlowIPM22`, `Mycielski`, `GenBank` |
| Mathematical subcollection | Algebraic/combinatorial boundary, incidence, relation, or design matrices | the 16 `JGD_*` groups, imported from Jean-Guillaume Dumas's Sparse Integer Matrix Collection |

Documented examples make the convention concrete:

- `HB` is the original Harwell–Boeing collection, so it is a historical archive,
  not one application.
- `Bai` is explicitly “matrices from Z. Bai, D. Day, J. Demmel, and J.
  Dongarra”; its name is provenance.
- `Sandia` is a circuit-simulation collection from Sandia National
  Laboratories.
- `LPnetlib` is a translation of Netlib linear-programming instances, whose
  principal matrix is the rectangular constraint matrix in `Ax=b`.
- `DIMACS10` is the 10th DIMACS Implementation Challenge corpus for graph
  partitioning and clustering; it deliberately combines real and synthetic
  graph sources.
- `VDOL` consists of KKT matrices generated during mesh-refinement iterations
  in optimal-control solves at the Vehicle Dynamics & Optimization Lab.
- `FlowIPM22` consists of graph Laplacians arising in Newton steps of a max-flow
  interior-point method on Chimera graphs.
- `JGD_Homology` contains matrices for simplicial-complex homology and is one
  of the `JGD_*` mathematical subcollections.

## What the matrices themselves represent

Across groups, the primary sparse array can encode several different objects:

1. **Discretized operators or linear systems.**  Finite-element,
   finite-volume, and finite-difference simulations produce stiffness, mass,
   Jacobian, saddle-point, or coupled-physics matrices.  Structural mechanics,
   CFD, thermal, electromagnetics, acoustics, semiconductor, and material
   problems dominate this category.
2. **Optimization systems.**  Linear-programming groups commonly store a
   rectangular constraint matrix; nonlinear/optimal-control groups often store
   a square symmetric-indefinite KKT/Newton matrix.
3. **Graphs and networks.**  Here `A(i,j)` records an edge or its weight.
   Undirected graphs normally yield symmetric patterns/values; directed graphs
   do not.  Bipartite graphs can appear as rectangular incidence-like arrays.
4. **Model reduction/eigenvalue problems.**  The primary `A` may be only one
   operator in a larger problem with mass/input/output matrices included as
   auxiliary metadata; the primary matrix's visible properties do not describe
   the entire dynamical model.
5. **Algebraic/combinatorial constructions.**  Boundary/differential,
   incidence, relation, design, and polynomial-system matrices are often
   rectangular and integer-valued.
6. **Least-squares, inverse, imaging, and data matrices.**  Interpolation,
   bundle adjustment, tomography, recommendation, document, and statistical
   problems can be strongly rectangular and may include multiple right-hand
   sides or labels in the problem metadata.

The official `kind` field is the best high-level application label.  It is a
qualitative curation field, as are `isND` (2D/3D discretization) and `isGraph`;
it is not inferred solely from algebraic matrix properties.

## How homogeneous are groups? Quantitative answer

The following counts are calculations from the 2,904-row public CSV, not claims
copied from the descriptions.  “Kind” below collapses only the curator's
`subsequent`, `duplicate`, and `sequence` qualifiers.  “Dimension” means
`max(nrows,ncols)`.

| Axis | Group-level result | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| Application kind | 172/203 groups have one normalized `kind`; 31 have more than one | Most groups are application-coherent, but the broad legacy collections are important exceptions. The 31 multi-kind groups contain 1,083 matrices (37.3% of the collection). |
| Shape | 159 square-only; 34 rectangular-only; 10 mixed | Shape is usually homogeneous within a group. The ten mixed groups are `HB`, `MathWorks`, `Bates`, `Pajek`, `Barabasi`, `Meszaros`, `JGD_BIBD`, `JGD_Homology`, `JGD_Margulies`, and `Hardesty`. |
| Exact numerical symmetry | Among groups containing square matrices: 61 all symmetric/Hermitian, 76 none, 32 mixed; 34 are rectangular-only | Symmetry is usually consistent, but 32 groups mix symmetric and nonsymmetric square members. |
| Real/complex domain | 184 real-only; 9 complex-only; 10 mix real and complex | Complex participation is usually homogeneous. This coarse split treats binary and integer matrices as real. |
| Exact curator value type | 39 groups mix two or more of `real`, `integer`, `binary`, and `complex` | Binary/integer/real distinctions reveal more within-group variation than the coarse real/complex flag. |
| Exact curator structure | 36 groups mix two or more of symmetric, Hermitian, skew-symmetric, unsymmetric, and rectangular | This is the most direct answer to “same matrix structure?”; numerical self-adjointness is also reported separately because complex-symmetric is not Hermitian. |
| Size | 60 have one dimension, 31 span ≤4×, 60 span 4–100×, and 52 span >100× | Scale is the least safe property to infer from group membership. The >100× groups contain 2,137 matrices (73.6%). Fifty-one of the 60 “one dimension” cases are single-matrix groups. |

Thus the typical pattern is **same provenance and often the same domain/form,
but potentially very different scale**.  Groups built from iterative solves or
mesh refinement are intentionally size sequences.  Historical and data-corpus
groups can be diverse on every axis.

## Important interpretation traps

- **Group is not `kind`.** `HB` is a source archive; “structural problem” is a
  kind.  A group can contain many kinds, and the same kind occurs in many
  groups.
- **Do not treat a surname as a domain.** A contributor may have supplied
  unrelated applications.  `Bai` is the clearest example.
- **Do not equate “same group” with “same size.”** Many of the best-curated
  homogeneous groups are refinement/time-step/parameter sequences and thus
  have enormous size ranges.
- **Do not equate symmetric pattern with symmetric numbers.** The official
  `pattern_symmetry` asks whether both `(i,j)` and `(j,i)` are present;
  `numerical_symmetry` also requires conjugate-equal values. `FIDAP` shows the
  difference.
- **For complex matrices, numerical symmetry means Hermitian.** The statistic
  compares `A(i,j)` with the complex conjugate of `A(j,i)`.  A complex-symmetric
  (but non-Hermitian) matrix can therefore have a value below one.
- **Rectangular matrices have symmetry statistics set to zero by definition.**
  They should be reported as “not applicable,” not lumped into nonsymmetric
  square systems.
- **`posdef=0` is not uniformly a proof of indefiniteness.** The documentation
  says `1` means known positive definite, `0` known not positive definite, and
  `-1` may be used for symmetric cases where definiteness is unknown, but the
  compact CSV/index treatment and graph conventions warrant checking the
  individual problem metadata before drawing solver conclusions.
- **A graph matrix is not automatically a linear system.** Its diagonal may be
  irrelevant and its structural rank may not even be computed; graph benchmark
  groups should be selected separately when evaluating linear solvers.
